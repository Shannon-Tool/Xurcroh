# 03. Threat Model

<Evidence type="limitation">Security boundaries are explicit</Evidence>

Xurcroh considers independently operated workers, network carriers, relays, and the controller potentially malicious. A worker may inspect its own weights, memory, and computation. The controller must not receive payload decryption keys. The trusted client owns boundary weights and session secrets.

## Assets and boundaries

- Plaintext prompts, source images/audio/video, and decoded outputs belong to the client.
- Full unprotected model weights must not appear on one worker.
- Inter-stage tensors require authenticated encryption and replay protection.
- Request-scoped KV, activations, latents, recurrent states, and transport buffers require lifecycle cleanup.

## Adversaries not eliminated

Colluding workers can combine their observations. Malicious computation can return incorrect results. Global observers can study endpoint identity, timing, and traffic volume. Physical host access can expose Python/framework copies, hardware registers, or attack attestation roots. Padded MoE traffic still has timing and microarchitectural side channels.

<Equation tex="\text{Obfuscation} \neq \text{FHE} \neq \text{Secret Sharing} \neq \text{Zero Knowledge}" />

The threat model therefore separates computational obfuscation, transport confidentiality, individual-worker custody, collusion, hardware-backed confidentiality, and formal proof. See [Security Evaluation](/17-security-evaluation) for the Procrustes and embedding-norm experiments that demonstrate these limits.
